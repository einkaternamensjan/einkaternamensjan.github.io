# Moths in Cloths

A tunnel. A dark tunnel. A long tunnel. Light. Bright light. White light. 
A tunnel. A bright tunnel. A short tunnel. Light. A dark light. Black light.

Speaker 1: I've been standing here for weeks. Is there really nothing else to do just now? 
Speaker 2: You've been standing here for weeks? Why? Have you got a screw loose?
Speaker 1: No. I've just been standing here for weeks. I don't know where to go. 
Speaker 2: Then there must be something else to do. 
Speaker 1: Hmm. You seriously believe there's something else to do? 
Speaker 2: What do you mean, believe? I'm not falling for that! 
Speaker 1: I don't know. I've been standing here for weeks. 
Speaker 2: ... 
Speaker 1: How long have you been standing here? 
Speaker 2: Good question. Let me count. *Counts carefully on his fingers*
Speaker 1: I've been standing here for weeks. 
Speaker 2: I'm standing. That's as far as I've come. No further yet. Speaker 1: Well, quite. But that's the purpose of standing. Not to get any further. 
Speaker 2: Hmm. You're right there. *stops counting* 
Speaker 1: If I wanted to go somewhere, then I'd go somewhere. *Paces 3 steps forwards*
Speaker 2: But you just went somewhere?
Speaker 1: What!? I didn't go anywhere. 
Speaker 2: Oh but you did. You just paced three steps ahead. 
Speaker 1: No. That can't be right. Because I remember very well that I in fact went nowhere at all. Speaker 2: Yes yes! You took three steps, so you've advanced. 
Speaker 1: No! That isn't right. And why do you put it like that? Ad-vanced?
Speaker 2: Because that's what you've just done, advanced! 
Speaker 1: But you're presupposing a goal, if you sum it up like that. 
Speaker 2: In what sense do you mean that now? 
Speaker 1: You say I've *ad*-vanced. But towards what? I haven't come to a goal.
Speaker 2: Now I don't understand you at all. You're just reasoning for the sake of it. But your point is void. 
Speaker 1: Oh forget it. My goal is standing about anyway, since there's nothing else to do just now. Speaker 2: You've advanced all the same. 
Speaker 1: I'll punch you in the mouth in a minute, you scoundrel! *Raises his fist* 
Speaker 2: What's that in aid of now? There's no understanding you, man. Really there isn't. 
Speaker 1: IN WHAT SENSE HAVE I *AD*-VANCED 
Speaker 2: Well, you took three steps. One. Two. Three. *imitates him* 
Speaker 1: But *towards what* have I come? 
Speaker 2: How do you mean that now? 
Speaker 1: You keep claiming I've *ad*-vanced. That flatly presupposes that I've come towards SOMETHING. 
Speaker 2: ... 
Speaker 1: But there's nothing here. Nothing except this cursed tunnel. 
Speaker 2: Aha! So you've got a problem with the tunnel! 
Speaker 1: Do you actually know how thick you are? 
Speaker 2: No. I bear no guilt in this. You're the one insisting on this nonsense.
Speaker 1: Forget it. *Walks four steps backwards* 
Speaker 2: Ok?
Speaker 1: What is it now? 
Speaker 2: You've done it again. 
Speaker 1: Done what? Do enlighten me. 
Speaker 2: Well. You've advanced. *imitates him*.
The Pedlar: *grins*
Speaker 1: How have I advanced?
Speaker 2: Through the four steps you just took. That's how you've advanced. 
Speaker 1: But man, I'm now in the very place I was at the start of this idiotic conversation.
Speaker 2: No. There you're lying wrong, no, standing wrong, even. Because this time you walked four steps. First three, now four. Three minus four equals minus one. So you've advanced. 
Speaker 1: But minus one makes no sense at all. Then I've just done the opposite of progress. So I've regressed. 
Speaker 2: But a step further on top of it. 
Speaker 1: But that's beside the point here. Because I have not *ad*-vanced. 
Speaker 2: Perhaps not from your point of view. 
Speaker 1: What do you mean, not from my point of view. From whose point of view have I advanced, for God's sake? 
Speaker 2: From mine. And from yours too, I think. You just don't know it yet. 
The Pedlar: *grins*
Speaker 1: And how would you know that? 
Speaker 2: I don't know anything, I'm only supposing. 
Speaker 1: You really are insufferable. Did you know that already? 
Speaker 2: So your goal is to get away from me? 
Speaker 1: From your buffoonery, perhaps... 
Speaker 2: Do you hear that? Speaker 1: Hear what? 
Speaker 2: The thunder? Speaker 1: Nah. 
Speaker 2: It'll probably be over again soon anyway. 
Speaker 1: Over again? 
Speaker 2: Yes. Why? Do you want to dispute that too? 
Speaker 1: You're imputing to the thunder, before it even existed, that it has already been over. Speaker 2: In what sense? 
Speaker 1: In that you say it'll soon be over *again*. But it's only just started. 
Speaker 2: Alright, fine. But I possibly meant thunder as such. Thunder in general. Not the one we're acquainted with just now. Plain thunder, you know?
Speaker 1: So you believe we only live in the pauses the thunder allows us? 
Speaker 2: Allows? An odd choice of words. But yes, roughly. 
Speaker 1: If I didn't know you, I'd take you for mad. 
Speaker 2: What does your knowing me change about the matter? Mad is mad, surely? 
Speaker 1: Well, I know you, so I *know* that you aren't mad.
Speaker 2: But how do you know that? I could just as well be mad, no, YOU could be the mad one here! It comes down to your perception of the matter. 
Speaker 1: That's true. Perhaps I have to… 
The Pedlar: *grins* 
Speaker 2: Perhaps you have to what? 
Speaker 1: Change my own position. 
Speaker 2: What for? 
Speaker 1: I can measure everything against the background. 
Speaker 2: How? 
Speaker 1: If I change my position. 
Speaker 2: I don't get it. Why would one have to change one's own position? 
Speaker 1: It's about the angle, man. 
Speaker 2: Which one? 
Speaker 1: I don't know exactly. But it's about an angle. 
Speaker 2: I understand that. Good. 
Speaker 1: If I shift my angle, no, shift myself, then I can see the world differently. Then I'll probably agree with you.
Speaker 2: That would be nice. 
Speaker 1: Come on, let's take a candle, light it, and by means of its light and our shadows we can observe our new positions. 
Speaker 2: Why have you got a candle? 
Speaker 1: Well it was dark when I entered the tunnel. I thought one would need a candle in here at some point. 
Speaker 2: But you've only got the one? There are two of us. 
Speaker 1: Damn and blast it!
Speaker 2: Two candles that would have to share one wick, that I could imagine. Two wicks for only the one candle is, on the other hand, ridiculous. 
Speaker 1: I think you're taking the piss out of me.
Speaker 2: Why? Why would I want to take the piss out of you? 
Speaker 1: Because you don't want me to observe your position. To have you placed.
Speaker 2: None of that is true. I want to go about this sensibly. And we can't do that with your paltry candle. We need something else. 
Speaker 1: So what do you suggest? 
Speaker 2: Your jacket, it's made of cotton, isn't it? 
Speaker 1: Yes, but what are you getting at? 
Speaker 2: Then let's set it alight! That'll light our tunnel enough for us to observe your position. Speaker 1: And you think the jacket would suffice for that? To illuminate our positions? 
Speaker 2: I don't know anything, I'm only supposing. It is to be hoped, though. 
Speaker 1: I think we don't want to observe it, but to measure it. Don't we? 
Speaker 2: Measure it? Against what? 
Speaker 1: Against the background? The wall, presumably? 
Speaker 2: Hold on, I stand in front of the wall, and you measure my position against the wall. Speaker 1: Good idea, the wall stays standing, but I don't. In that respect it's perfect! 
Speaker 2: Good then that's settled already.
Speaker 1: But how am I to remember where you were standing? I'll have to mark the outline on the wall somehow. 
Speaker 2: That's true, you're entirely right there. 
Speaker 1: I could mark your shadow on the wall. That would be enough, wouldn't it? 
Speaker 2: I should think so. Then afterwards we can look at the markings and calculate our differences.
Speaker 1: But with what? 
Speaker 2: Chalk, perhaps? 
Speaker 1: Yes! Splendid idea! 
Speaker 2: The pedlar's coming shortly. He ought to have some.
Speaker 1: Indeed. Otherwise he's no good as a pedlar. 
Speaker 2: Why would he have chalk? 
Speaker 1: Well, so he can sell it. To turn a profit. 
Speaker 2: Ohhh, you mean the pedlar.
Speaker 1: Yes exactly. The pedlar, the one who's coming. 
Speaker 2: Then we ought to wait for him. 
The Pedlar: *grinning, holds up a piece of white chalk*.
Speaker 1: When is he actually coming? 
Speaker 2: Soon, that's all I know.
Speaker 1: I hope it won't take much longer. 
Speaker 2: Are you in a hurry? 
Speaker 1: Nah, not that either.
Speaker 2: The pedlar comes, the pedlar comes, the whole street's bombed to bits and crumbs Speaker 1: … 
Speaker 2: My mother always used to sing me that. 
Speaker 1: Perhaps he'll have a torch as well, then we wouldn't need the candle any more. 
Speaker 2: Yes, but we're waiting for the chalk! 
Speaker 1: True, but if we have to wait anyway, then let's do it properly, no? Proper waiting! Speaker 2: As you like it. The Pedlar: *slowly draws a torch together with the chalk out of his coat, presents them both, grinning* 
Speaker 1: Then I could keep my jacket. 
Speaker 2: Perhaps. We can only hope he's got a torch. 
Speaker 1: Then let's just hope.
Speaker 2: When you measure my position, you must also hide it from me. 
Speaker 1: Why? 
Speaker 2: So that it doesn't influence me. You know? If I observe my own position, then I might feel the urge to change it, to move to a more favourable one. 
Speaker 1: That's true, good idea. But how would I do that? 
Speaker 2: Here, take your jacket back. Then you can cover my eyes with it, to guard me against my position. 
Speaker 1: Good! Then that's how we'll proceed. *He binds his jacket round Speaker 2's head, covering his eyes in the process, inspects the binding intently, so that no light gets through.* 
Speaker 2: *blinfolded* Wonderful! I can't see a thing. 
Speaker 1: I can only see a little more than you anyway. It's so dark in here. 
The Pedlar: *Shows himself to Speaker 1, offers him the chalk and the torch, goes on grinning, his eyes are wide, almost manic.* 
Speaker 2: Is the pedlar here already? 
The Pedlar: *Vanishes into the shadows, but remains at the edge of the scene*
Speaker 1: He was here. He offered me the chalk AND a torch!
Speaker 2: Did you have to pay for them? 
Speaker 1: Not yet. 
Speaker 2: Then we can begin!
Speaker 1: Indeed we can! *Switches the torch on, sets it on the floor and aims it at Speaker 2, whose shadow is thrown onto the wall, begins slowly and carefully to chalk Speaker 2's position onto the wall.* 
Speaker 2: How does it look? 
Speaker 1: It's alright, I'll go over it once more so it can be seen more clearly. *Chalks Speaker 2's outline onto the wall again, this time with zeal* 
Speaker 2: Right then, I'll go on standing here.
Speaker 1: Ok, now let's start on the second angle! Speaker 2: Perfect! 
Speaker 1: *Switches the light off, raises the torch by degrees and begins to beat Speaker 2 to death.* 
The Pedlar: *Laughs manically.*
Speaker 1: *Wipes the blood from his face, stoops down and begins to dissect the disfigured face of Speaker 2 with his hands*
Speaker 1: But this is pitiful. 
The Pedlar: *Silent, grinning.*
Speaker 1: *Takes a closer look at the smashes brain* 
The Pedlar: *Grins* 
Speaker 1: I've been standing here for weeks. Is there really nothing else to do just now? 
The Pedlar: *Grins* 
Speaker 1: *Stands up, strokes his chin with one hand, and in doing so gradually begins to weep.* 
The Pedlar: *Grins*
